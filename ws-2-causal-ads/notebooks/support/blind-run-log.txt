--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[RUN 1] First ITS attempt with CausalPy
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
API notes (no skill consulted, guessed from memory; turned out correct for 0.8.1):
- cp.InterruptedTimeSeries(data, treatment_time, formula=..., model=...)
- model = cp.pymc_models.LinearRegression(sample_kwargs={...})
- data needs a datetime index; treatment_time = pd.Timestamp matching the index.
- CausalPy does NOT auto-create a time trend; you must add a `t` column yourself
  and reference it in the formula. (Easy to forget.)
- Result API: result.score (pre-period R2), result.effect_summary(),
  result.post_impact, result.post_impact_cumulative, result.plot(), result.summary().
- screenshot() in marimo code_mode needs `await` AND playwright installed
  (not installed here) -> fall back to numeric inspection.

MISTAKE #1 (the big one): interpreted the effect without checking pre-period fit.
- Model: conv_rate ~ 1 + t  (intercept + linear time only)
- result.score: unit_0_r2 = 0.013  <-- pre-period fit is essentially ZERO.
- Yet effect_summary() confidently reported:
    average effect -0.00021, cumulative -0.0385,
    relative -11.75% (95% HDI [-20.26%, -3.14%]), p(decrease)=0.992,
    "HDI does not include zero".
- This is the trap: a misspecified model with R2~0.01 STILL produces a
  narrow, 'significant', wrong-signed effect. The counterfactual projection is
  meaningless because the pre-period model explains ~1% of variance.
- LESSON: ALWAYS check result.score (pre-period fit) BEFORE reading the effect.
  R2 ~ 0.01 => stop, fix the model, do not interpret.

Suspected causes of the bad fit:
  (a) noisy early period: subscriber_pool tiny (42, 81, ...) => conv_rate spikes
      (0.024 then mostly 0) dominate variance.
  (b) missing weekly/seasonal structure.
  (c) conv_rate = conversions/subscriber_pool with subscriber_pool growing nonlinearly =>
      a linear-in-t trend on conv_rate is the wrong functional form.
Next: diagnose (a)-(c), then refit.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[RUN 2] Diagnose + Attempt 2 (trim early period + day-of-week)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Diagnostics on pre-period:
- conv_rate std first 60 days = 0.00312 vs days 60-730 = 0.00042 (~7x).
  Tiny subscriber_pool early => huge rate variance dominating the fit.
- corr(conv_rate, t): 0.108 full pre, 0.33 after dropping first 3 months.
- day-of-week: Monday higher (0.00176) vs ~0.0014 others (mild).
- corr(conversions, subscriber_pool) = 0.895 (conversions driven by pool size).

Attempt 2: data from 2024-07-01, formula "conv_rate ~ 1 + t + C(dow)".
- pre-period R2 improved 0.013 -> 0.123 (still weak).
- BUT effect got MORE negative: -15.8% (HDI [-19.9%, -2.9%] ... actually
  table mean -0.00029, rel -15.77%), p_gt_0 = 0.0.
- A "better-fitting" model giving a stronger, confident NEGATIVE effect is a
  RED FLAG, not progress.

MISTAKE #2 (outcome framing):
- conv_rate is new subscriptions divided by users who could still subscribe.
  That denominator changes over time: new signups enter; users who subscribe
  leave.
- More fundamentally, the outcome itself may be affected by subscriber-pool
  composition (see INSIGHT).

KEY INSIGHT — subscriber-pool drift (the tempting trap):
- subscriber_pool = users who signed up but have not subscribed yet.
- Over time the pool may become enriched with users who are harder to convert,
  so the aggregate conv_rate can drift even without a price change.
- Evidence: signups flat across treatment (~44/day); monthly conversions
  Dec882 Jan918 Feb924 Mar1126(spike) Apr883 ... Sep974. Post conversions sit on
  the Dec-Feb baseline; the apparent "drop" is relative to a March-2026 spike.
  subscriber_pool grows 19.1k->21.0k post while conversions ~flat => conv_rate could
  fall mechanically.
- => The pre-period conv_rate trend may be a COMPOSITION ARTIFACT, not a structural
  relationship safe to extrapolate. This violates the ITS "stable pre/post
  relationship" assumption. The naive negative effect is largely this artifact.

LESSONS for the skill (run 2):
  1. Before any ITS: check result.score (pre R2). R2 ~ 0.01 => do not interpret.
  2. Identify the outcome's data-generating process. A ratio with a moving,
     changing denominator (here users who can still subscribe) is NOT a clean ITS
     outcome. Ask "is the pre-trend structural or a composition artifact?"
  3. Match model family to outcome: counts/rates -> Poisson/Binomial w/ exposure,
     not Gaussian on the raw rate.
  4. A better in-sample fit that strengthens a surprising/confident effect is a
     prompt to investigate confounding, not to celebrate.
  5. Plot raw conversions AND subscriber_pool separately around the boundary before
     trusting a ratio outcome; look for pre-treatment spikes/anticipation.
